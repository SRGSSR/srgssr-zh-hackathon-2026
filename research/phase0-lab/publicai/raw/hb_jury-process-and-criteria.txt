 Jury Process & Evaluation Criteria
 Pitching Process
 Evaluation takes place in two rounds on Friday, September 25 : 
 Round 1: Expert Jury (All Teams) 
 When: 14:00 – 16:00 (Submission deadline: 12:00 ) 
 Setup: Pitches take place in a closed room across 3 parallel panels. 
 Pitch Time: 4 minutes total (1-min pitch + 3-min Q&A). 
 Submission: Via form link (link to be published) . 
 Goal: Shortlist selected teams for the main stage (announced at 17:00 ). 
 Round 2: Main Jury (Selected Teams) 
 When: 18:00 – 18:45 (Final submission deadline: 17:30 ) 
 Setup: Pitches take place live on the main stage. 
 Pitch Time: 3 minutes total (2-min pitch + 1-min Q&A). 
 Submission: Via email (details to be published) . 
 Goal: Team visibility, final winner selection, and prize distribution at the Award Ceremony ( 19:30 ). 
 *Please note the pitch timing
 In the Expert Jury, you have 4 minutes ( 1 min pitch and 3 min Q&A)
 In the Main Jury, you have 3 minutes ( 2 min pitch and 1 min Q&A)
 Jury Rules
 Eligibility: The jury only evaluates projects submitted on time by registered teams where all members have a valid ticket and ID. 
 Independence: The jury acts independently to select winners. Decisions are final and cannot be legally appealed.
 Conflicts of Interest: Any juror with a bias or personal interest in an outcome must declare it immediately and recuse themselves from voting. 
 Moderator: The jury moderator holds the tie-breaking vote on all decisions. 
 Evaluation Criteria
 Judging is qualitative. You do not need to provide formal quantitative proof. 
 Preferences
 Clean, reproducible code is preferred.
 Concepts and execution that leverage modern AI paradigms.
 For challenge submissions, the challenges objectives should be addressed.
 Criteria 
 These criteria only structure the jury's discussion. The jury as a group may choose winners at its own discretion.
 Technical Functionality & AI: Does the solution work? Is the implementation technically sound and reproducible?
 User Experience: Is the solution intuitive, useful, and easy to use?
 Agentic Depth: Do autonomous agents handle complex logic?
 Originality & Fun Factor: Is the solution novel, surprising, or creatively engaging?
 Potential & Market Impact: Does it address a relevant problem? Can it grow beyond the hackathon? If applicable, does it meet the challenge objectives?
